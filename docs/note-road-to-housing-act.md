## Section 208, Innovation Fund

The provision that matters to this challenge.

**What it is.** A HUD competitive grant program for local governments that have increased
local housing supply.

**Eligible entity.** A metropolitan city or urban county, or a unit of general local
government or Indian tribe, as defined in section 102 of the Housing and Community
Development Act of 1974 (42 U.S.C. 5302), **that has demonstrated an objective improvement
in housing supply growth**, as determined by the Secretary. KCMO qualifies as a
metropolitan city, subject to the supply-growth test.

**Qualifying initiative, quoted from the Act:**

> "(vi) streamlining regulatory requirements and shortening processes, increasing code
> enforcement and permitting capacity, reforming zoning codes, or other initiatives that
> reduce barriers to increasing housing supply and affordability"

**Terms:**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum grant | $250,000 |
| Maximum grant | $10,000,000 |
| Grants per year | Not fewer than 25, with spread across rural, suburban, urban |
| Authorization | $200,000,000 per fiscal year, FY2027 through FY2031, inflation-adjusted to CPI-U |
| Program stand-up | Not later than 1 year after enactment |
| Sunset | 7 years after enactment |

**Priority.** The Secretary gives priority to an entity that has demonstrated "the use of
innovative policies, interventions, or programs for increasing housing supply" and "a
marked improvement in housing supply growth."

**Application requires:** a description of each purpose; data on characteristics of
increased housing supply over the 3-year period ending at application; how each purpose
addresses a community need or advances an objective in the consolidated plan; and a
description of how the entity "has carried out, **or is in the process of carrying out**,"
qualifying initiatives.

**Rule of construction.** Nothing in the section authorizes HUD to mandate, supersede, or
preempt any local zoning or land use policy.

### What this means for the challenge

1. **Eligibility turns on housing supply growth, not on permitting improvement.** Running
   the challenge does not make KCMO eligible. Demonstrated supply growth does. The
   permitting work earns *priority* once eligible. Do not let this be misstated internally.

2. **The challenge counts while it is running.** The application accepts initiatives
   "in the process of carrying out." A challenge underway is describable.

3. **Baseline measurement now carries a funding rationale.** The Stage 0 requirement to
   measure current QCR cycle time, resubmittal and rework rate, reviewer consistency, and
   first-pass completeness was justified on evidentiary grounds: without it, Gate 5 is
   unfalsifiable. It is now also the documentation that makes a Section 208 application
   legible. Same work, second reason.

4. **Watch the Federal Register.** HUD must publish its housing-supply-growth methodology
   for **at least 90 days of public comment before the first notice of funding
   opportunity**. That is both an early warning of the NOFO and a comment opportunity.

5. **Scale.** The floor of the grant range is roughly four times the $60,000 the sponsoring
   department is funding for Stage 1.

## Section 209, Accelerating Home Building Act

Adjacent, and worth knowing for a predictable question.

Grants to units of general local government, municipal membership organizations, and
tribes to **select prereviewed designs**, which the Act defines as:

> "sets of construction plans that are assessed and approved by localities for compliance
> with local building and permitting standards to streamline and expedite approval
> pathways for housing construction"

Also called pattern books. Funds may **not** be used for construction, alteration, or
repair. Grantees report on impacts, designs selected, **the number of permits issued for
housing development utilizing prereviewed designs**, and units produced. Ten percent rural
set-aside. HUD may require repayment if designs are not adopted within 5 years. Up to
5 percent for technical assistance, including pre-grant assistance to prospective
applicants.

**Scope note.** Section 209 covered structures are low- or mid-rise, not more than 25
dwelling units, and include accessory dwelling units, infill development, duplexes,
triplexes, fourplexes, cottage courts, courtyard buildings, townhouses, multiplexes, and
other structures with **not less than 2 dwelling units**. The CPD challenge scope is one-
and two-family. Duplexes and ADUs overlap. Detached single-family does not.

**Why it matters to the challenge.** Pattern books and AI-assisted screening solve
different halves of the same problem. Prereviewed designs remove review effort from repeat
designs. Screening speeds review of custom submittals. They are complementary, not
alternatives. Expect to be asked why the City is not simply adopting pattern books.

## What is not in the Act

**No mention of artificial intelligence.** Zero occurrences in the enrolled text. The Act
neither assists nor constrains the AI governance work, and nothing in it bears on the DC
or California procurement material.

## Other provisions noted in passing

- **Point-access block buildings.** HUD to issue model code language, best practices, and technical guidance to states, territories, tribes, and localities to facilitate permitting; to engage the International Code Council toward incorporation into the International Building Code; and may establish competitive grants for pilot projects evaluating safety, feasibility, or cost-effectiveness. Applies to Group R-2 occupancy up to 6 stories with a single internal stairway. Not within the CPD challenge scope.
- **Sec. 103.** Exemption on construction or modification of residential housing located on an infill site.
- **Sec. 210.** RESIDE Act, converting empty structures to housing.
- Twelve titles overall, including community banking, manufactured housing, program reform, veterans, oversight, and a Central Bank Digital Currency prohibition.
