# Governing Authority: KCMO Administrative Regulation 3-27 — Innovation Partnerships

**City of Kansas City, Missouri**
**The local authority and legal vehicle under which every public AI innovation challenge is run**

---

## Why this document exists

The PPG panel was explicit that the federal precedents (FAR, DARPA, NASA COTS, SBIR)
are *reference models only:* **"For local government, these are reference models. Our
authority still comes from our own charter, ordinances, and procurement rules."**

**AR 3-27 is that authority.** It is KCMO's own, already-adopted policy for testing,
evaluating, and demonstrating innovative solutions with outside partners — and it
supplies the exact thing the federal set could only analogize to: a **local legal
vehicle** (the Innovation Partnership Agreement) that lets the City stage commitment,
open its data and facilities temporarily, and walk away on performance.

The [Framework](./framework.md) is *how* to run a rigorous, AI-appropriate, staged
challenge. **AR 3-27 is what makes it lawful and executable at KCMO.** The framework
operates *inside* AR 3-27, not beside it.

**criteria**, and the **selection process** for innovation partnerships.

## 2. The vehicle — the Innovation Partnership Agreement

Per §3.1, the City may enter an **Innovation Partnership Agreement** to test, evaluate,
and/or demonstrate innovative solutions that meet AR 3-27's goals and criteria — and
**"may make available temporary use of City owned resources and infrastructure
including but not limited to land, facilities, equipment, right of ways, and data."**

This is the local analogue to NASA's Space Act Agreement: a flexible, non-standard
partnership instrument, distinct from an ordinary procurement contract, that authorizes
**temporary, scoped access to City data and infrastructure** for a defined test. It is
the legal container for the framework's staged pilot.

### Partnership types (§4.1) and how an AI challenge classifies

| AR 3-27 type | Definition | Relevance to an AI challenge |
|--------------|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Pilot Project** (4.1.1) | A product/process/service/IT **already available** in the U.S. marketplace the City may explore. | Use when adopting a mature, market-available AI tool. Follows Procurement Division procedures (§6.3.1). |
| **Innovation/Testing** (4.1.2) | An outside party asks to use City land/facilities/ROW/equipment/**data** to test/evaluate/demonstrate its innovative solution. | **The typical vehicle for an AI innovation challenge** — the City opens representative data so teams can demonstrate. |
| **Partnership Development Opportunity** (4.1.3) | An Innovation/Testing partnership the City flags as a candidate for economic-development contribution and/or City-use benefit. | Applies when a challenge also advances AdvanceKC / local-innovator goals. |
| **Innovative Solution** (4.1.4) | A product/process/service/IT **not yet in the U.S. marketplace, or improving an existing one**, expected to deliver measurable benefits and containing **"a specific and identifiable performance component."** | What most AI challenges seek. Note the built-in demand for a *measurable performance component* — the hook for the framework's gates. |

> **Terminology caution:** AR 3-27's "Pilot Project" means *a marketplace-ready tool*,
> while the framework's "Controlled Pilot" (Phase 2) is a *stage of the challenge*. In
> agreements, name the AR 3-27 **type** (usually *Innovation/Testing* seeking an
> *Innovative Solution*) separately from the framework **phase**.

## 3. Goals and criteria (feed Gate 0)

**Goals (§5.1)** — a partnership should achieve one or more of:
- Create new markets/jobs or support existing local innovators;
- Improve quality and efficiency of City services and operations;
- Attract and retain top talent;
- Improve economic vitality, social equity, and environmental quality.

**Also required:** all **Council Priorities** are considered (§5.2); both parties' goals
are stated openly and the partnership is mutually beneficial (§5.3).

**Evaluation criteria (§6.3.2)** — the Partnerships Team evaluates on:
1. **Consistency** of the partnership's goals with Council Priorities, mission, and core services;
2. **Balance** of potential benefit to the City against potential cost to the City.

These are precisely the questions the framework's **Gate 0 (Problem & Authority)** must
answer. AR 3-27 supplies the local content of G0.

## 4. Governance — the Innovation Partnerships Team

AR 3-27 §6.2 names a standing body the City Manager designates to coordinate all
innovation partnerships:

| Team composition (§6.2) |
|-------------------------|
| City Manager's Office · Office of the Mayor and City Council · Finance · Law · General Services – Procurement Division |

**Team responsibilities (§6.2):** implement the policy; advise departments on
interpretation; review/develop partnership agreements; **track and report all
partnerships**; act as the contact and connect partners to departments; **recommend
approval to the City Manager**; and report to City Council. The Team meets at least
quarterly.

**Approval authority (§6.3.3):** agreements are **recommended by the Team and approved
by the City Manager.**

> This is the outer accountability ring around the framework's roles (§6). The
> Department AI Lead's advance/remediate/stop recommendations flow up through the
> Partnerships Team to the **City Manager**, who holds final approval. A challenge does
> not "self-authorize" — it is recommended and approved through this chain.

## 5. Process and performance measures

**Process (§6.3.1):** every potential partnership is forwarded to the Team, which
identifies the type. Pilot Projects follow Procurement Division procedures;
Innovation/Testing projects first get an **initial cost/benefit assessment** prepared by
the partner (accounting for staff time, out-of-pocket cost, **risk to the City**, and
lost opportunity of providing core services).

**Performance measures (§6.4):** *"At the outset of each Innovation Partnership
Agreement a set of performance measures should be established by both parties. At the
conclusion of each project, a report evaluating the project against the stated
performance measures … should be forwarded to the Innovation Partnerships Team."*

> This single requirement is the seed of the whole framework. AR 3-27 already demands
> **measures set up front and evaluated at the end.** The framework **operationalizes
> and strengthens** it: instead of one measure-at-start / evaluate-at-end cycle, it
> installs a **staged gate ladder (G0–G5)** so evidence is checked *between* commitments,
> not only after. The framework does not contradict AR 3-27 — it is a rigorous
> implementation of §6.4.

## 7. What AR 3-27 does *not* cover — and what supplements it

AR 3-27 is a **general** innovation-partnership policy from 2013. It is the authority and
the vehicle, but it is intentionally generic. Three gaps must be filled by the framework
and its reference models:

| Gap in AR 3-27 | Filled by |
|----------------|-----------|
| **No AI-specific provisions** — nothing on model transparency, bias/equity testing, training-data disclosure, model versioning, or AI data-rights. | Framework **Governance Readiness pack (§7.2)** + the **D.C. AI Procurement Handbook** content (risk tiers, vendor disclosure, monitoring clauses). |
| **Single-point evaluation** — measures at start, report at end (§6.4); no staged checkpoints before commitment grows. | Framework **staged gate ladder (§5)** and monotonic-commitment invariant (§1) — the federal staging precedents (FAR 39.103, SBIR, COTS) inform the discipline. |
| **Light on solicitation format** — no challenge-announcement structure or scoring model. | Framework **§5–§7** + the **Challenge-Based Acquisition** announcement outline; **15 U.S.C. §3719** for announcement elements and judge-conflict rules. |
| **Data/IP terms not fully specified for AI.** | Framework **§8** fixed terms (city controls data/results; vendor keeps background tech; no training unrelated models; portability/exit), written into the Innovation Partnership Agreement. |

**Net:** AR 3-27 authorizes and houses the challenge; the framework makes it
AI-appropriate and evidence-staged; the federal and municipal references supply battle-
tested detail for the parts AR 3-27 leaves open.

## 8. Compliance checklist for a challenge run under AR 3-27

1. **Classify the partnership type** (§4.1) — usually *Innovation/Testing* seeking an *Innovative Solution*; flag as a *Partnership Development Opportunity* if it advances AdvanceKC.
2. **Forward to the Innovation Partnerships Team** (§6.3.1) before engaging partners.
3. **Prepare the initial cost/benefit + risk assessment** (§6.3.1) — feeds the framework risk tier and Gate 0.
4. **Confirm Gate 0 against AR 3-27 criteria** (§6.3.2): Council-Priority/mission/core-service consistency, and benefit-vs-cost balance.
5. **Establish performance measures up front** (§6.4) — express them as the framework's gate thresholds and scoring weights.
6. **Execute the Innovation Partnership Agreement** as the vehicle; attach the §8 data/IP terms.
7. **Route advance/stop decisions through the Team to the City Manager** (§6.3.3) for approval at each commitment increase.
8. **File the concluding evaluation report** against the stated measures with the Team (§6.4); the Team tracks/reports and reports to Council (§6.2).
