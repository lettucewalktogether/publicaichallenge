## 1. Announcement and purpose

City Planning and Development (CPD) invites qualified teams to submit proposals for a
public AI innovation challenge addressing the **Quality Control Review (QCR)** process and
**residential plan review for one- and two-family homes**.

The challenge will test whether AI-assisted tools can improve completeness screening,
review consistency, traceability, and staff efficiency under City-defined conditions.

The City intends to select up to three teams for a 45-day co-design and demonstration
stage. Each selected team will assign forward-deployed AI engineers to work with City
subject-matter experts. Based on documented performance, the City may invite one or more
teams to a controlled pilot of up to six months.

### 1.1 No guarantee of further work
Selection for Stage 1 does not guarantee advancement to Stage 2 or any future City action.
The City may end the challenge after either stage. The City may advance no team, one team,
or more than one team.

### 1.2 Stipend and participant contribution
The Stage 1 stipend of $20,000 per team provides **partial** support for the required
challenge activities and accepted deliverables. The stipend may not cover a participating
team's full cost. Each team is responsible for determining whether and to what extent it
will contribute additional personnel, technology, or other resources. **The City will not
reimburse costs beyond the stated stipend.**

### 1.3 Separate City decisions
Acceptance of Stage 1 deliverables, payment of the Stage 1 stipend, advancement to
Stage 2, and any future City action are **separate decisions**. Completion of Stage 1 does
not guarantee advancement, a pilot, procurement preference, or future work.

### 1.4 No production authorization
Neither stage authorizes production deployment unless the City provides separate written
approval.

## 2. Background

The City is evaluating practical uses of AI-assisted tools in two related areas:

1. **QCR intake and completeness review**
2. **Residential plan review for one- and two-family homes**

The City seeks to determine whether qualified teams can help staff identify missing
information earlier, improve first-pass screening, reduce avoidable review cycles, and
prepare clearer review information.

> **AI output will support staff work. It will not replace City review, professional
> judgment, code interpretation, or permitting authority.**

### 2.1 The City's review framework
CPD's residential review framework includes **Information Bulletins IB100, IB115,
IB171-RE, and IB172**, addressing one- and two-family dwelling submittals, express review
pathways, and residential energy-code compliance. These materials provide an established
basis for controlled testing under real review conditions, real submission expectations,
and measurable outcomes.

## 3. Challenge objectives

### 3.1 QCR intake and completeness review
- Identify documents included in a submission
- Identify required documents or information that appear to be missing
- Compare submitted materials with applicable intake requirements
- Organize findings for staff verification
- Distinguish clear deficiencies from issues requiring staff judgment

### 3.2 Residential plan screening
- Screen plan sets for common review issues
- Identify potentially missing or inconsistent information
- Connect proposed findings to applicable City requirements
- Direct reviewers to the relevant drawing, page, detail, or document
- Refer uncertain or complex conditions to staff

### 3.3 Consistency and workflow support
- Support consistent treatment of similar submissions
- Prepare standardized comments where appropriate
- Maintain traceable findings and an auditable review record
- Reduce repetitive screening without creating excessive verification work
- Fit within the current QCR and residential review workflow

### 3.4 Human-centered adoption and organizational readiness
- Engage affected staff in structured discovery, workflow design, testing, and evaluation
- Identify changes to roles, responsibilities, review practices, training, supervision, and applicant communications
- Measure whether staff can understand, verify, modify, reject, and explain AI-assisted findings
- Determine whether the solution reduces work, shifts work to another role, or creates new verification burdens
- Evaluate whether the workflow can be sustained without continuous vendor involvement
- Distinguish problems addressable through technology from problems requiring policy, process, staffing, training, or organizational change

## 4. Challenge structure

The challenge consists of two stages. **Advancement is discretionary and based on
documented results.**

| Stage | Purpose | Duration | City decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — Co-design challenge** | Configure and test initial feasibility, performance, and workflow fit | 45 days | Whether any team should advance |
| **Stage 2 — Controlled pilot** | Validate performance under broader operational conditions | Up to 6 months | Document results; determine next steps |

### 4.1 Purpose of the phased structure
The phased structure allows the City to increase its commitment as evidence develops.
Stage 1 tests a limited set of use cases under controlled conditions. Stage 2, if
authorized, examines operational performance, governance readiness, staff adoption, and
sustainability under broader conditions.

## 5. Stage 1 — Co-Design Challenge

### 5.1 Purpose and limits
Stage 1 combines structured co-design with a formal demonstration under common
City-defined test conditions. The City will evaluate **working capability and team
performance**. Vendor-selected demonstrations and written descriptions alone will not
satisfy the challenge requirements.

**Existing or substantially developed capability.** Respondents should propose an existing
or substantially developed capability that can be configured for the defined use cases.
Stage 1 is not intended to fund development of a new enterprise platform from the ground
up.

Stage 1 does not require production deployment, enterprise integration, or completion of
all documents required for a controlled operational pilot. Deliverables must be
proportionate to the 45-day duration and the defined use cases. Stage 1 tests whether a
proposed capability warrants further evaluation; it is not intended to establish
readiness for production deployment.

### 5.2 Forward-deployed AI engineering
Each selected team will assign qualified technical personnel — which may include
forward-deployed AI engineers, solution engineers, implementation engineers, applied AI
specialists, or personnel performing comparable functions — to work directly with
designated City staff throughout Stage 1 to:

- Observe and document current QCR and residential review workflows
- Translate approved City source materials into testable system requirements
- Configure prompts, rules, retrieval methods, models, integrations, and output formats
- Test the solution with City-provided materials and review errors with staff
- Identify conditions in which the system should defer to staff
- Document data flows, security controls, third-party services, and dependencies
- Train designated City staff to use and evaluate the prototype
- Maintain a configuration, issue, and decision log

*The City will evaluate assigned personnel based on the functions they perform and the
results they produce, rather than on a specific job title.*

### 5.3 Human-centered discovery and adoption activities
- Conduct focused discovery with designated QCR staff, plan reviewers, supervisors, and other affected participants
- Document the current workflow, affected roles, decision points, common exceptions, verification needs, and recurring sources of delay or rework
- Conduct limited usability testing to determine whether designated staff can understand, verify, modify, reject, and explain prototype findings
- Identify adoption barriers, work that may shift to another role, and process or policy decisions that should be addressed during a pilot
- Demonstrate that designated staff can complete defined prototype tasks without continuous vendor direction

### 5.4 Co-design responsibilities and boundaries
City staff will provide operational knowledge, approved source materials, representative
examples, and feedback. Each team remains responsible for technical design, configuration,
performance, documentation, and compliance with challenge requirements.

**City participation does not constitute approval for production use or acceptance of
vendor technology. City staff retain responsibility for official code interpretation and
permitting decisions.**

The City will provide reasonably comparable materials, staff access, and co-design
opportunities to participating teams. Material clarifications affecting the challenge will
be documented and distributed as appropriate.

### 5.5 Stage 1 scope boundary
Before Stage 1 begins, the City will provide a **challenge package** defining the work to
be performed, identifying: included application and project types; included QCR and
residential review activities; applicable Information Bulletins and City-provided
requirements; number and general complexity of development cases; number and general
complexity of blind evaluation cases; anticipated file types, sizes, and plan-sheet
ranges; required prototype inputs and outputs; expected format for findings and source
references; planned co-design sessions and anticipated staff availability; permitted data
classifications and approved access methods; activities and integrations excluded from
Stage 1; and conditions for requesting clarification or City revision of materials.

**Unless expressly included in the challenge package, Stage 1 does not require:**
integration with the City's production permitting system · applicant-facing deployment ·
automated communication with applicants · production data migration · enterprise identity
integration · payment-system integration · unattended automated decision-making.

The City will not require a participant to complete materially expanded work outside the
defined Stage 1 scope without a written amendment or other authorized adjustment.

### 5.6 Stage 1 work plan

| Step | Activities | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Workflow observation, staff interviews, process mapping, issue identification | Current-state workflow and issue summary |
| **Requirements translation** | Map approved sources to testable functions, controls, and outputs | Use-case and requirements document |
| **Configuration and iteration** | Configure the prototype, test development cases, document changes | Working prototype and change log |
| **Controlled evaluation** | Run common and blind test cases under defined rules | Comparable performance results |
| **Findings and pilot plan** | Present results, limitations, dependencies, recommended pilot scope | Final report and pilot work plan |

### 5.7 Minimum Stage 1 deliverables
The City will evaluate whether each deliverable is complete, responsive to the defined
scope, and sufficiently documented for City review. **Acceptance of deliverables is
separate from the comparative decision about advancement.**

1. **Workflow and user-needs summary** — current process, affected roles, decision points, recurring problems, verification needs, conditions requiring City judgment
2. **Configured prototype** — working solution configured for the defined QCR and residential review use cases
3. **Requirements traceability matrix** — tested functions connected to applicable Information Bulletins and City-provided requirements
4. **Controlled test results** — correct findings, missed findings, incorrect findings, uncertainty referrals, and results by issue severity
5. **Technical disclosure** — proposed architecture, data flow, models, third-party services, hosting approach, significant dependencies
6. **Human-centered test findings** — usability, staff verification time, accepted and rejected findings, workflow exceptions, observed adoption barriers
7. **Limitations and risk summary** — known limitations, unsupported uses, prohibited data practices, conditions requiring deferral to staff
8. **Proposed Stage 2 plan** — pilot scope, milestones, staffing approach, City resource needs, major assumptions, and principal cost drivers including expected licensing structure, third-party charges, usage-based fees, integration assumptions, data storage charges, support assumptions, and any functions requiring separate licenses

*Pricing provided during Stage 1 may be preliminary and nonbinding unless the challenge
documents state otherwise.*

## 8. Stage 2 — Controlled Pilot

Stage 2 may test a successful Stage 1 solution under broader and more realistic operating
conditions. Activities may include shadow review, controlled use on selected application
types, comparison with completed staff reviews, testing on previously unseen submissions,
limited integration, and measurement of staff verification time.

### 8.1 Pilot milestones
1. **Pilot readiness** — complete security review, configuration, access controls, training, and data controls
2. **Controlled validation** — meet defined accuracy and error thresholds on a City-controlled test set
3. **Shadow operation** — process selected submissions alongside staff without independently generating official decisions
4. **Workflow assessment** — measure staff time, consistency, turnaround, usability, and operational burden
5. **Operational readiness** — document integration, support, governance, maintenance, and transition requirements

### 8.2 Human-centered pilot and change-management requirements
- Designate a departmental sponsor, process owner, Department AI Lead, and operational decision-makers before pilot launch
- Provide role-based training before staff use the system, and refresher training when the system or workflow materially changes
- Operate a staff feedback channel and recurring review sessions to identify usability, workload, trust, and process issues
- Test applicant-facing comments for clarity, accessibility, consistency, and unintended burden
- Maintain an adoption dashboard covering usage, verification, overrides, errors, escalations, and workflow effects
- Document lessons learned and revised procedures before any decision about continued use

### 8.3 Pilot measures
Accuracy and errors by issue type and severity · staff verification time and total
review-cycle time · changes in avoidable resubmittals and reviewer consistency · system
availability, latency, and operational reliability · security, privacy, accessibility, and
records compliance · staff effort, system demands, and resource requirements · performance
when City requirements or source materials change.

### 13.2 Human oversight and accountability
Identify the intended form of human oversight for each function and document who may
review, approve, override, suspend, or terminate system activity. Support a responsibility
matrix covering the departmental sponsor, process owner, Department AI Lead, supervisors,
staff, technology, security, legal, procurement, and vendor personnel.

### 13.3 Equity and accessibility
Test performance variation across relevant submission types, drawing conventions, file
quality, project conditions, and applicant pathways. **Do not infer protected
characteristics from plan documents or use them as decision factors.** Ensure staff and
applicant-facing outputs meet applicable accessibility and plain-language requirements.

### 13.4 Public records and auditability
Make AI outputs, system logs, reviewer actions, source references, evaluation records, and
decision records exportable and retainable as required by City policy and Missouri
public-records requirements.

### 13.5 Incident response and suspension
Provide procedures for identifying and reporting incidents, suspending affected functions,
preserving records, correcting affected outputs, supporting investigation, and defining
restoration criteria.

### 13.6 Prohibited capabilities and secondary data use
Disclose prohibited, latent, or configurable capabilities and provide written controls
preventing unauthorized activation. Do not sell, license, share, commercialize, retain
beyond authorized periods, or use City data or derived data to train unrelated models.

## 14. Human review and decision authority

1. **AI output is advisory.**
2. **Authorized City staff retain responsibility** for official review findings and permitting decisions.
3. Staff must be able to **accept, modify, or reject** AI-generated findings.
4. The system must **identify uncertainty and defer** unsupported conclusions.
5. Material findings must be **traceable** to submitted documents and approved City requirements.
6. The system must preserve an **auditable record** of output and staff action.
7. Production use, if any, would require defined **monitoring, escalation, and change-control** procedures.
